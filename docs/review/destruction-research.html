Glass that gives, furniture that fails, walls that leak — how shipped games break things, priced against this renderer

A survey of Control, Rainbow Six Siege, Source's window glass, impact-placed fracture research and real glass forensics, mapped item by item onto a box-only ray-traced office where destruction is already bounded and hash-deterministic and the guards see and hear through the player's own light, smoke, sound and floors. Ends in a staged plan, five crossfire set-pieces for the "Seoul" framing, and ten one-line decisions.

"stuff like the seoul mission works really well too, where we're the quiet one sneaking around when there are two loud teams going at it — keep that in mind for chaos and destruction! i think we'd want the beats to be pretty heavily scripted but the actual destruction, etc., should be dynamic"

The three that pay

Most wow per millisecond in this renderer, where every photon is dynamic and moving light is the thing it does that nothing baked can:

A fixture shot loose, swinging F2

One end of a corridor panel (or the storage bulb) drops out and hangs by its flex, still lit and stuttering: penumbrae sweep the floor, the froxel beam swings through the gypsum dust, the gloss lino mirrors it. The light meter swings with it, so Sam crosses in the dark half of the period and the guards' sight of him breathes in and out through the same meter.

≈ 0 ms · one moving light; ReSTIR is flat in light count

Panes coming apart cell by cell G1–G3

Each round stars the pane; the stars link into a hairline Voronoi network of the impacts; on the third or fourth the cell round a cluster drops out as tinted specular shard quads that flash through the torch beam and tinkle onto the lino — and the hole is a new path for rounds, sound and the storm, both ways.

≈ 0 ms persistent · shards at the timer quantum, births-capped

Light through fresh holes in drywall W1

Sustained fire opens lattice-aligned holes between box studs; the lit corridor stabs into the dark storage room through a ragged row of them, beams standing in the dust. Sight, fire, sound and light all leak through real geometry — murder holes for everybody, walkable for nobody.

a few boxes · GI settles through the hole in ~0.5 s

The one recurring cost to police is smoke-solver density (gypsum dust, a CO₂ cloud): +2 ms per hanging plume at high, already gated by smoke.budget() and puffDomains. Everything else on this page is particles, decals, a handful of boxes, or a light that moves.

What shipped games do

Control · Northlight GDC 2020 [1][2][3][4]

"Granularity": nature as "a continuum from very big objects down to dust and very thin veils of smoke", drawn in three overlapping layers — rigid bodies ("chunks and parts of props and environments — all of that is RBDs"), a middle layer of "mesh particles, rigid body hierarchies, and material decals", and sprites for "embers, sparks, smoke, fire, splinters, gravel, sand".

Reactions authored per material, not per object ("concrete breaks into smaller concrete bits and then spawns dust; shoot a metal pipe and it spawns water") by a Houdini pipeline tagging props with material metadata; props are part hierarchies on breakable joints, "so if you break a table in half the parts fall and collapse realistically". Reported budget: ~200 live dynamic objects, overflow chunks demoted to sprites, collision throttled a few frames after a blast (second-hand numbers [4]).

Take: the layering and the cap-with-demotion are already the shape of destruction.ts (pool 64/32 → refusal → chip burst). What we lack is Control's middle layer for furniture: parts on joints.

Rainbow Six Siege · RealBlast GDC 2016 slides [5][6]

Surface destruction is planar: "3D → 2D projection", "arbitrary cutting polygons cut a planar surface", the cut pattern from "impact position in local space + inputs and material parameters (cutter)", Weiler–Atherton clipping, ear-clipping triangulation, extruded back to 3D. Cutter classes: perimeter (ellipse, spline), inner fragments (Voronoi), both — "Glass, Texture". Under it a "connection-based leaf graph".

Their performance calls: "no procedurally cut dynamic fragments — well-placed replacements, instanced, recycled aggressively; fragments don't collide together; vaporize on explosion; collision always boxes"; ~6 ms budget for a wall, a bullet hole 0.33 ms PC / 1.1 ms PS4; async + time-sliced. Determinism: "seed a RNG … based on impact position". Systems made dynamic: navlinks, "sound (& propagation)", "AI visibility through partially broken walls".

Take: the closest engineering match to the ask — and glass.ts hitSeed(bi, point) is their determinism rule verbatim. Our panes are already 2D analytic surfaces; we do the cut in the shader, not in a mesh.

Source func_breakable_surf shipped code [7]

A window = a grid of ≤ 16 × 16 panels of 12 units. A bullet shatters the hit panel, the neighbour if within 0.2 of an edge, "occasionally" the one or two above; blasts take a radius. Then BreakThink recomputes support per intact panel as a weighted sum of neighbours (frame = full support: bottom 1.25, sides/top 1.0, lower diagonals 1.0, upper 0.25, ÷ 6.75), iterating; anything under 0.20 × fragility drops as a falling pane or shatters in place — the tongue between two hit clusters follows a beat later. Each survivor is drawn with an edge style from the bitmask of broken neighbours.

Take: the fondly remembered precedent for "shatters after a few bullets, based on where they landed". Crude, deterministic, free — the bar to clear, and its support pass ports directly onto Voronoi cells.

Fracture placed at the impact [8][9][10][11][12]

Müller–Chentanez–Kim 2013: pre-authored fracture patterns applied at the impact location at run time (plain pre-fracture's flaw: "the pattern does not align with the impact location"), partial fracture at several places. Blast/APEX productise the model: chunk hierarchy, support graph of bonds with health, "damage shaders" (radial, shear), islands when bonds break, optional stress solver — and "no physics … no graphics representation". Chaos: Voronoi uniform / clustered / radial, cluster levels, thresholds, anchor fields. FEM (DMM) is the serious end.

Take: keep the data model (bonds, health, islands, radial damage) for furniture; drop the geometry libraries — we have no meshes to cut.

Real glass SWGMAT [13] · glass types [14]

Radial cracks first, from the impact (initiating on the far, tensioned face); concentric second, "usually in straight segments that terminate in an existing radial crack"; a fast projectile leaves a cone/crater wider on the exit side; and "the sequence of multiple impacts can be deduced when the cracks caused by a subsequent impact terminate at previously formed cracks" — several bullets partition a pane into cells bounded by each other's cracks.

Annealed breaks progressively into large sharp shards; tempered holds, then dices all at once into cubes once its skin is breached; laminated spider-webs and stays in the frame. Three behaviours from one parameter.

Take: a Voronoi-of-impacts crack network is physically defensible, and glass type is the authored knob that makes the lobby frontage, the conference partition and the manager's sidelight behave differently.

Contrast, not candidates [15]–[19]

Teardown: voxels with an 8-bit material palette, bodies split by connectivity, multiplayer as "a stream of deterministic commands — 'cut hole in this shape at voxel coord x,y,z'" in fixed point. Red Faction: Guerrilla: a stress walk over supports across frames, chain-reacting. Frostbite: "remove a piece of the geometry → detail meshes → destruction mask (albedo + normal via a distance field, drawn as a deferred decal) → particles and mesh debris". The Finals: the whole sim server-side. Noita: every pixel, 64² chunks.

Take: determinism-by-command and decal masking we already have; voxels, stress solvers and server authority solve problems a 1 m grid of ≤ 4 096 boxes does not have. skip

The glass model

One analytic pane, an impact list, and the Voronoi diagram of the impacts doing three jobs: the crack network you see, the cells that fail, and the hole test every ray, round, footstep-sound and gust asks.

Mapped onto this engine

Priced against: a tumbling shadow-casting box ≈ 0.03 ms at high (P6: 8 → 64 boxes = 8.5 → 9.9 ms union), a hanging dust domain +2 ms high / +2.5–4 ultra, particles at the 65 µs quantum with births capped 384/frame, decals free (512 ring), 782 statics + ~300 dynamics of MAX_BOXES 4 096 repacked per frame, 23 of MAX_GLASS 32 panes at ~22 slab tests per glass pixel, and StaticCollision skipping NoShadow statics at query time — hiding a static un-blocks sight, sound, rounds and shins in one flag write.

Destruction in a scripted crossfire

Beats scripted, breakage dynamic. Who breaches which door when, where the fight sets up, when the mains go: squad.ts order() / Guard.script / BreachDrill / qrf.ts / setBlackout on a timeline. What breaks is only ever fireWeapon → destr.round / glassHit and detonateStun → destr.bang / crackNear from real rounds the sides (operative / hostile / guard) fire at each other — so a pane fails where their bursts actually landed, two runs of one beat break different things, and a seeded capture still repeats. Every item above was also judged on does it read from across the room while Sam watches and does it hand him an opportunity.

1

Conference-room siege

Hostiles hold the conference room; operatives stack on its corridor door (the breach drill verbatim) while a second pair suppresses from the corridor's east end. Their rounds star the six partition panes, cells drop between the mullions, shards shower onto the lino under the panels and leave a crunching patch across Sam's obvious east–west route; the holes make the partition a two-way firing line (both AIs' clearShot now pass) and carry corridor footsteps into the room. Sam needs the manager's office beyond: cross behind the stack during the bang, or go round through the cubicles.

glass 1a–1c · suppression · BreachDrill · tactics doorGate — worst case 6 panes × 16 impacts, ≤ 6 failures × ~50 shards spread over frames; persistent GPU ≈ 0; the muzzle gas and flashes are the real cost and already in the loud minute

2

Cubicle bay push

The QRF element comes through the west stair door and pushes east through the cubicles at hostiles falling back on the break room. Tracers shred partitions, paper storms lift and re-lift under each bang, monitors die screen by screen so the bay gets darker as the fight goes on (Sam's concealment improves through the meter), gypsum dust off the corridor wall hangs in the torch cones (the sight-line probe honours it — measured: detection through it drops to a seventh–two fifths for seconds), a tile lets go; with stage 2 a pedestal gives and a desktop tilts its binders onto the floor, landings blamed on the shooters so Sam's debrief stays clean.

destruction as built + impulses + F1 — the measured +2–3.5 ms at high while dust hangs; debris ≤ 64; the decal ring turns bullet holes over after ~40 s of sustained fire (oldest first)

3

Lobby, lights out, one lamp swinging

Scripted: operatives cut the mains at T+40 s (beacons). Dynamic: a hostile burst had already shot a lobby fixture loose, so one lit panel swings on its flex over the reception stone, its pool sliding back and forth in the gloss; a stray burst takes a tempered frontage pane whole — 300 cubes glittering under the sodium lamps — and the storm gusts in through the opening (papers, spray, the moths scatter), wet prints following whoever uses it. Sam crosses in the dark half of each swing.

F2 · G5 · blackout · wind/weather · motes — ≈ 0 GPU beyond the blackout itself; one wind source

4

Server-room lift under someone else's breach

Sam wants the drive; operatives breach the manager's office next door with a stun (panes star, papers fly, the tile above drops), hostiles answer down the corridor; a stray round holes the corridor extinguisher and a cold white cloud rolls along the floor past the server-room door — eight seconds of concealment Sam did not have to make (or he makes it himself with one suppressed round and eats the hiss).

F3 · destruction bang path · devices (pad_side) — one smoke domain, +1–2 ms for ~10 s

5

Break-room garnish

The vending front cracks and stutters dark, cans roll out clattering and pull the one calm guard left on the floor off the fire exit; the cooler bottle finally lets go into a puddle that mirrors the muzzle flashes.

F5 · F4 — ≈ 0 / +0.1–0.3 ms while the puddle is in view; optional, cheap, funny

Bounded when two squads empty magazines for a minute (≈ 15 rounds/s sustained): heat saturates by construction; decals 12/s + holes turn the 512 ring over in ~40 s; puffs ≤ 8/s inside puffDomains 6; knock-offs 4/s; debris ≤ 64/32 with eviction; glass ≤ 16 impacts × 32 panes, shards through the births cap and the LONG ring; tiles ≤ 8; drywall holes ≤ 16; hanging fixtures ≤ 2; extinguisher 1. Nothing allocates; everything hashes a serial.

Decisions (one line each)
  1. GLASS_FAIL_HITS — interior annealed: first cell drops on the 3rd hit within 0.5 m (else the 4th anywhere)? tempered exterior dices on the 5th? laminated (sidelight, clerestory) never opens?
  2. Reverse the 08-09 call explicitly: panes MAY open again — as cells, never the old whole-pane pop — yes / no.
  3. Do guards read wreckage as evidence (a shattered pane / fallen desk in a calm room → suspicious, like an open door), or stay ears-only as today?
  4. Shards underfoot louder for EVERYONE (guards announce themselves on their own breakage) — confirm the one-model reading even when it helps Sam.
  5. Dangling fixture: may a second burst cut it down (light dies, fixture becomes debris), or does it always keep swinging lit?
  6. Extinguisher cloud: one per encounter, 8–10 s, priced like a smoke can — fine inside the loud minute's domain budget, or only when smoke.budget() ≥ 6?
  7. Stage 3 changes the level format and makes level-identity stale until the generator retires — green-light now, or park W1 behind 1b / 2b?
  8. Crossfire set-pieces: a third showcase first (?crossfire, like ?breach) on the timeline runner, or straight into the mission as a tier above the QRF?
  9. Bullet-hole turnover under a minute of two squads (512 ring ≈ 40 s): acceptable, or raise the ring to 1 024 (buffer ×2, same single binding)?
  10. Tempered dice count per pane — 200 / 300 / 400 cubes: sodium-lamp glitter vs the LONG ring's turnover.
