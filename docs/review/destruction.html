twopointsix · stage 3b · lane feat/destruction

Destruction under sustained fire, behind one knob

Hold the FN2000's trigger across a desk and the office now comes apart in the order a real one would: the monitor dies and goes over, the paper is in the air, gypsum dust fills the panel light and the torch beams — dust the guards' eyes really lose you in — the mugs and binders leave the desk, and if you keep it up a ceiling tile lets go overhead and lands with a knock the floor hears. All of it sits behind tune.chaos (Tab ▸ Sandbox ▸ chaos, default 0.5): at 0 the game is byte for byte the game it was, and above 0 every path is a fixed pool or a hard cap.

Rounds — anybody's — and stun charges pour heat into a 2 m grid with a 2 s memory. A deliberate two-rounds-a-second semi cadence saturates at heat 4: it dents and scatters and raises dust but never knocks anything off at 0.5 and never drops a tile at any level under 1. The tour pins the knob to 0 (the showcase is choreographed round the office as authored).

The look

A desk raked on full auto

The tan cluster's north-east desk from the north, chaos 1, 24 rounds in 2.4 s (docs/shots/destr_paper_*): the lit monitor dies on the first round and its housing goes over, both paper stacks are in the air by the third, gypsum dust off the spine partition by the ninth; three seconds after the trigger goes quiet the leaves lie on the desk and the floor, the neck stub stands, the shreds show on the partition.

Dust in a torch beam

Thirty rounds into the corridor plaster beside a fixed torch (destr_dust_*): the dust the rounds knock out of the wall is smoke-solver density, so the froxels light the beam's shaft through it and it thins over the following seconds; the chips carpet the lino, a tile from the beaten ceiling lies by the wall.

A ceiling tile lets go

A stun charge's worth of destruction on the aisle by the carton (`__destr.bang`, no flash), captured every 0.1 s: the tile see-saws out of the plenum casting its shadow, crumbs beside it, and lands flat with a knock the floor hears (`prop` 0.4).

A monitor dies

The lit screen on the red cluster's desk (destr_monitor_*): one round — the emissive goes through its light target the same frame (the cell it lit goes dark), a crack decal, a fizz of sparks; then a burst knocks the housing off the neck and a tile down onto the desk.

Sheets are particles, so they cast no shadows and are not geometry once down: forty shadow-casting quads per stack is exactly the fast small occluder in hard light the image-quality program said to keep out, and a reverse conversion (a settled leaf back to a thin static) needs an asynchronous readback that would break the module's determinism. They lie there for their 60 s life; the LONG ring recycles the oldest only under load.

Cost

M4 Max, Chrome/Metal, serialized frames (each followed by onSubmittedWorkDone + a yield). The GPU was shared with other sessions on the machine for every take of this lane — the baseline itself reads 4–5 ms over the image-quality program's numbers for the same preset and p95 carries contention spikes that show at chaos 0 too — so the conclusions rest on interleaved MEDIANS and on deltas, and the absolute bars (high ≤ 14 · ultra ≤ 30 in the loud minute) cannot be re-derived here. What the deltas say: while a burst lasts and for the few seconds its dust hangs, chaos 0.5–1 costs +2 ms union at high and +2.5–4 at ultra over the same burst at chaos 0 — inside ultra's headroom, and at high enough to cross the loud minute's bar by a millisecond or two for those seconds if the auto ladder does not trim first.

Where it goes: the dust in the smoke solver is the price — not the solver itself (fullRateDomains held it flat in every take) but the direct pass's shadow rays and the gather's rays sampling transmittance through the extra domains, plus the froxel injection over them (+0.2). Residuals
  • Sheets cast no shadow and stay particles once down; a leaf resting over a desk edge lies flat in the air past the edge (its centre is supported); leaves piled at one height merge in tone but for the rim.
  • Dust puffs are density only — the field has no per-emitter albedo — so gypsum, concrete and wood dust are the plume's grey-white, told apart only by the particle burst's tint in the first half second.
  • The ceiling slab is NoPrimary (the camera looks through it): a dropped tile leaves no visible hole and tiles are generic 60 × 60 slabs; a square under a light panel or a beacon is refused rather than shattered.
  • Knock-offs are indexed by shape, never by name (the level file carries none): 28 clutter + 8 binders qualify — mugs, phones, the jug and glasses, the desk fan, plant pots, the photo frame, the wall clock; keyboards (40 cm) and shelf cartons do not; chairs spin but never topple (3-DOF props).
  • Game state depends on the graphics tier once the debris pool saturates (64 vs 32: chunk vs chip burst decides whether a landing is heard) — accepted and documented; the pool never saturated in any take.
  • Guards do not read wreckage as evidence — only what they hear land and what the dust hides.
  • The stress-minute bars need a quiet GPU to re-derive; the deltas are what this page can vouch for.

What a kinetic mode would raise, on the same budgets

  • a per-domain thin-medium hint so shadow / gather rays skip puff-only domains under a density floor (the one real cost);
  • the debris pool used toward its measured 64-mover budget with authored break-offs (Voronoi-cut partitions, drywall panels) instead of whole statics, and monitors / chairs as 6-DOF bodies;
  • sheets and chips as occluders in ultra's mesh-shadow BVH; the reverse paper conversion on a deterministic readback schedule;
  • guards treating a wrecked room like an open door — suspicion on sight.
