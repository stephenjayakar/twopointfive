Smoke without boxes

What it would take for smoke to stop living in eight fixed boxes, what each way costs, and a prototype that does it. Benched twice on your Mac, and since 10 Oct it is the smoke: the boxes are deleted. Section 08 is new.

branch adaptive-smoke-grids · no switch · handed to the merge, not yet in the build to play

Both rendered by the real game page at medium, 640 × 360, on a software GPU in the cloud, so they're small and noisy. Shapes are trustworthy, image quality isn't.

The short version

Points 1 to 9 are in the order things happened, so "today" in them means the eight boxes. Point 10 is where it stands now.

  1. It's possible on raw WebGPU, and the plain version is the right one. Not an octree, not adaptive resolution. Small bricks of today's voxels, placed wherever smoke is, on one lattice that covers the whole level, drawn from one fixed pool. It's what NVIDIA Flow does, and by all accounts EmberGen and Houdini's sparse pyro too.
  2. I built it, and it runs. Same solver maths, every reader ported: the view march, shadow rays, froxels, the guards' sight lines. Walls, a shut door and a ceiling vent all act on it, each checked. Glass is wired the same way as walls, not checked on its own. It boots clean in headless Chromium.
  3. We never actually decided against this. There's no written rejection anywhere. It was put off four times, three of them in your own words, and one unmeasured note called it "unlikely" on WebGPU for want of frame time. The API turns out not to be the obstacle, and going by your bench neither is frame time.
  4. What goes away: the count (twelve cans at once all live, today four of them are refused soft GPU), the faces where smoke ends, plumes that can't merge, and 24 rules in the game that only exist to live with the boxes counted.
  5. What replaces it is a cap on total smoke volume. And here's the finding that matters. The box walls were quietly deleting smoke, and that was holding the cost down. Let a can spread on the open floor and 12 s in it fills 1,825 bricks, 3.6 boxes' worth soft GPU. On your Mac, with the vents blowing, it peaks at 2,029 bricks 34 s in, four boxes' worth your Mac. So a wisp gets cheaper, a fresh plume, or a stun and a shot, comes out about even, a can that's had time to spread in the open gets dearer, and it covers a lot more floor. That's a change to how the game plays, on top of how it looks.
  6. You said you'd let quality drop where you aren't looking, and that you'd rather not have to think about it. Bricks make that easy, because every brick knows where it is. The first piece is in: when the pool gets tight, smoke far from where the camera is looking thins first and is refused first, and the last quarter of the pool is kept for what's near it. Twelve cans at once, pool full: the three near the camera kept 87% of the bricks a lone can gets and all of its smoke, the four furthest gave up about a quarter of theirs soft GPU. No per-level tuning, nothing for game code to count.
  7. What it costs on your Mac: less than I'd guessed, and its worst case is under today's. With the pool full the solver takes 2.16 ms and smoke adds 3.00 ms to the frame. Today's eight boxes take 2.83 and add 3.68. One to four fresh cans cost 0.4 to 0.5 ms more than today. The dear case is one can left to burn out in the open: about 1.5 ms more than today from 26 s on, for three times the volume of smoke. All your Mac, high, 734 × 770 internal, one run each. My estimate had been 3.1 to 7.4 ms for the full pool.
  8. You said finish it, so I finished it on the branch, behind a switch at the time. 01What the smoke does today, and where the limit comes from

All of this is read from the code at ef91484 (the build to play when I branched), not remembered.

  • Eight boxes, each 64³ cells, allocated up front. MAX_DOMAINS = 8, DIMS = [64, 64, 64] (src/smoke/smoke.ts:84, :71). Nine 3D textures of 64 × 64 × 512, 112 MiB whether one box is live or eight counted.
  • Two voxel sizes, so two box sizes. 2.2 cm for gun gas makes a 1.4 m cube. 5.5 cm for cans and dust makes a 3.5 m cube, floor to ceiling.
  • A box never moves and boxes don't talk. Nothing carries density or velocity from one to the next. Each snaps to its own lattice. Where two overlap the readers just add them.
  • Smoke that reaches a face is deleted. The five open faces hold pressure at zero, the last six cells dissipate smoke at 0.7/s rising to 30/s (smoke.wgsl:378), and every reader fades the outer 11% of the box so the end isn't a plane (common.wgsl SMOKE_EDGE). That fade is the soft straight edge you still see.
  • The ninth plume is refused, or the least important one is recycled. Silently (smoke.ts:337-360, stats.refused). Smoke more than 15 cm above the ceiling line is refused too, so nothing upstairs.
  • Each live box is its own chain of dispatches, 11 plus two per pressure sweep pair: 19 at high, so up to 152 in a row with eight live counted.
  • The rest of the game is tuned round the boxes. I counted 24 rules in game and shader code that exist only because of the cap or the box: destruction stops opening dust boxes at six so cans and gun gas keep two (destruction.ts:64), each shot's gas is anchored 38 cm ahead of the muzzle so it fits one fine box (effects.ts:186), dust tint and the 1.3× render density are per box, not per puff. All 24 are listed with their lines in rebuild/smoke-grids/consumers.md, section 3.4.

Why eight, why a pool? No reason is written down anywhere. It is already eight in the first commit that holds the file (6d5a1dc, 08-05). The 64³ size does have a reason, and it's budget: 76cb760 (08-06) says the bigger boxes were "paid for by the cheaper pressure solve". No commit since has changed the count, moved a live box, grown one, merged two or coupled two.

03What's possible, and what each way costs

I read what ships and what's published (NVIDIA Flow 2's source, the DCGrid, Cirrus and Fire-X papers, Dawn's Metal backend, the WebGPU spec; most other hosts were blocked from here, so some numbers are second hand and tagged so). Three things came out of it.

  • "No domain limit" never means unbounded. Flow and GVDB are the same thing, and so by their own descriptions are EmberGen 2 and Houdini's sparse pyro second hand: uniform blocks over one huge virtual lattice, drawn from a fixed pool. The cap is on total active voxels. There's none on count, shape or place.
  • At our size the clever data structures are dead weight. The whole level is 91 × 19 × 64 bricks at 5.5 cm. A flat table does what hashes, trees and octrees do in the papers counted.
  • WebGPU's traps are about dispatches, not data. No sparse textures, no bindless, ten storage buffers a stage, a serial Metal compute encoder, and Dawn slips a validation dispatch in front of every indirect dispatch source read. All survivable. I found no public WebGPU sparse smoke demo to copy search only.

04The prototype

Branch adaptive-smoke-grids, switch ?sparsesmoke, read once at boot. Off is today's solver with every shader text, layout and bind group as it was. Nothing is merged anywhere.

It's the same solver step, equation for equation (semi-Lagrangian velocity, vorticity confinement, red/black SOR pressure, MacCormack density, the same emitters, wind, door leaves and glass as obstacles). What changed is where the cells live.

  1. One lattice over the whole level per voxel size, cut into bricks of 8³ cells: 44 cm bricks at 5.5 cm, 18 cm bricks at 2.2 cm. A flat page table says which bricks exist.
  2. One pool of 4,096 bricks, the same 2.1 million cells the eight boxes hold. Both voxel sizes draw from it.
  3. Each step, on the GPU: emitters and bricks holding smoke claim themselves and whatever neighbours the smoke could reach next step. Bricks nothing has claimed for 20 steps go back on the free list. No CPU read-back in the loop.
  4. The solver runs over the list of live bricks, whatever plume they belong to. Each brick knows its 26 neighbours, so stencils and backtraces cross borders directly and pressure sweeps need no border copies.
  5. Bricks with smoke are published into a texture atlas with one cell of border, plus a small 3D page texture. So every reader keeps one hardware trilinear fetch, after one extra lookup. Shadow rays and the guards' sight lines walk the page texture brick by brick and skip clear air.
  6. When the pool runs low it fails soft: from 60% full, thin smoke decays faster and the "call it zero" threshold rises. A brick that is refused becomes an open face with today's dissipation band, just there. All of that starts sooner and bites harder away from where the camera looks, and the last quarter of the pool is kept for smoke near it.

05What it did

Everything in this section is soft GPU unless it's tagged otherwise: the real game page in headless Chromium, medium preset (three pressure pairs), steps of 1/30 s, render density 1, calm stepping off, ceiling vents off, in both solvers. No run on either solver raised a GPU validation error.

"Smoke in the air" below is density times litres, summed over every cell. The game ships cans at render density 1.3, and the prototype lays that in at emission, so in play expect somewhat more bricks than these runs show.

A standard smoke can at the same spot in the cubicle aisle, run for 12 s in each solver.

The start of a real room clear

I logged every smoke emit from a headless run of the four-man room clear (84 emits in 20 s: two stun grenades, eight shots, some wall dust) and played the first ten seconds into the prototype: one stun grenade, then one shot 3.6 s later. The grenade's cloud takes 268 bricks at once and 689 three seconds on. The single shot's gas takes about 190 more within half a second and about 400 four seconds later, because gun gas lives on the fine lattice, where a brick is only 18 cm across. Together they peak at 1,299 bricks and have only eased to 1,232 ten seconds after the bang. No brick was refused. Today those two events would hold one coarse box and one fine box, 1,024 bricks' worth counted. So for firefight smoke it's roughly even, not the saving I expected. The win there is the missing cap: headless missions hold 0.2 to 2.1 boxes on average but 4 to 8 at the worst moment headless (rebuild/performance/measured.md:870), and that's where today's refusals come from. I stopped at ten seconds for time, so the second grenade and the seven other shots aren't in this.

How far to trust it

  • Boot: 111 of 111 pipelines compile, no validation errors, with the switch on. With it off the page builds the same 107 pipelines from the same shader text as before.
  • Two independent reviews read every kernel, every reader and the off path line by line. They found no race, no out-of-bounds access and no way to lose smoke on the GPU side, and called the off path clean. They did find host-side bugs: smoke vanishing in scripted frame bursts, wind sources picked without regard to where the smoke is, a stale read-back after a reset. Those are fixed and re-checked. Something I learned on the way. The can's smoke is authored to fade at 0.14 a second. Stepped every frame it really fades at 0.30. The other half is the solver itself: every step smears thin smoke a little, and what drops under a floor is zeroed. So smoke that steps less often lasts longer. That's where the drift above comes from, and it should mean thin smoke hangs longer at 30 fps than at 60, in today's boxes too inferred.

What keeps the seams quiet: a brick takes the shortest wait among itself and its 26 neighbours, a source wakes everything it reaches on every frame, whole columns step together, no wait is longer than a twelfth of a second, and pressure is stored per unit of time so bricks on different clocks can read each other. Whether any of it shows is the first thing on the look list.
